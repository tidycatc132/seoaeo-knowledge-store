Summary: Google explains how it estimates the date shown in results and how to help it. Says it uses several factors, not one.
## Passages
### P1 definition
> A byline date is the date that Google estimates that the web page was updated or published.
### P2 not single
> Google doesn't depend on a single date factor because all factors can be prone to issues.
### P3 significantly updated
> look at several factors to determine our best estimate of when a page was published or significantly updated.
### P4 must describe
> The dates must describe the publication or update date of the page, not the stories or events described therein.
### P5 future dates
> Don't specify future dates, or the date of the action described on the page.
### P6 visible date
> Add a user-visible date to the page and feature it prominently.
### P7 structured data
> specify the datePublished and/or dateModified fields.
