Summary: Click study (with eye tracking) showing clicks are informative but biased by trust in the ranking, and that relative preferences derived from clicks are reasonably accurate. Research on click interpretation, not evidence of what Google ranks on.
## Passages

### P1 informative but biased
> clicks are informative but biased

### P2 trust bias
> there is a trust bias which leads to more clicks on links ranked highly by Google

### P3 biased by trust
> they are biased by the trust they have in the retrieval function

### P4 relative preferences
> we show that relative preferences derived from clicks are reasonably accurate on average

### P5 match explicit judgments
> which are shown to correspond well with explicit judgments
