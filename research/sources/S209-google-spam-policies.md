Summary: Google's spam policy defines keyword stuffing as filling a page with keywords to manipulate rankings.
## Passages
### P1 definition
> Keyword stuffing refers to the practice of filling a web page with keywords or numbers in an attempt to manipulate rankings
### P2 unnatural
> Repeating the same words or phrases so often that it sounds unnatural.
### P3 cloaking
> Cloaking refers to the practice of presenting different content to users and search engines
### P4 cloaking example
> Showing a page about travel destinations to search engines while showing a page about discount drugs to users
### P5 link spam
> Link spam is the practice of creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings.
### P6 automated links
> Using automated programs or services to create links to your site
### P7 scaled
> Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings
### P8 genai
> Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value
### P9 site reputation
> The site reputation policy applies where third-party content is published on a host site mainly
### P10 thin affiliation
> Thin affiliation is the practice of publishing content with product affiliate links
### P11 consequence
> Sites that violate our policies may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all.
### P12 detection
> We detect policy-violating practices both through automated systems and, as needed,

### P14 thin affiliation full
> Thin affiliation is the practice of publishing content with product affiliate links where the product descriptions and reviews are copied directly from the original merchant without any original content or added value.
### P15 site reputation full
> The site reputation policy applies where third-party content is published on a host site mainly because of that host's already-established ranking signals, which it has earned primarily from its first-party content.
### P16 expired full
> Expired domain abuse is where an expired domain name is purchased and repurposed primarily to manipulate search rankings by hosting content that provides little to no value to users.
### P17 doorway definition
> Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries.
### P18 doorway intermediate
> They lead users to intermediate pages that aren't as useful as the final destination.
### P19 doorway regions
> Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page
### P20 doorway similar pages
> Creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy
### P21 doorway funnel
> Generating pages to funnel visitors into the actual usable or relevant portion of a site
### P22 scaled continued
> and not helping users.
### P23 buying links
> Buying or selling links for ranking purposes
### P24 goods for links
> Exchanging goods or services for links
### P25 product for link
> Sending someone a product in exchange for them writing about it and including a link
### P26 link exchanges
> Excessive link exchanges ("Link to me and I'll link to you") or partner pages exclusively for the sake of cross-linking
### P27 directory links
> Low-quality directory or bookmark site links
### P28 footer links
> Widely distributed links in the footers or templates of various sites
### P29 normal economy
> Google does understand that buying and selling links is a normal part of the economy of the web for advertising and sponsorship purposes.
### P30 qualified links ok
> It's not a violation of our policies to have such links as long as they are qualified with a rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored" attribute value to the <a> tag.
